# Customer Discovery Playbook — Nudge Reach

_Goal: before pouring effort into building/scaling, get hard evidence that real Indian shop owners have the problem we think they have and will pay to solve it. This is a 2–3 week sprint, not a permanent program._

---

## 1. The hypotheses we're testing

Write these down so a conversation can actually falsify them.

- **H1 (Problem):** Small retailers want to market to their existing customers on WhatsApp but find it too fiddly/time-consuming to do well.
- **H2 (Today's behavior):** They currently broadcast manually (personal WhatsApp, Status, copy-paste to groups) or do nothing.
- **H3 (Wedge):** "Take a photo of a product → get a finished, send-ready campaign" is compelling enough to make them switch/start.
- **H4 (Willingness to pay):** They will pay ₹500–1,500/month + message costs for it.
- **H5 (Compliance/risk):** They don't know or care about Meta compliance — so "we keep you compliant automatically" is a *reassurance*, not a *selling point* (test this).

**Validated = ** at least **8 of 12** target interviews independently confirm H1+H2, **5+** react strongly positive to the photo wedge (H3), and **3+** verbally commit to paying or pre-pay a deposit (H4).

---

## 2. Who exactly to talk to (ICP for discovery)

Tight beats broad. Start with one or two verticals so the demo and messaging are sharp.

**Primary ICP — "visual-product" small retailers in Tier-1/Tier-2 India:**
- Apparel & textiles (sarees, dupattas, boutiques, ethnic wear) ← strongest fit; the seed demo is a Banarasi silk dupatta.
- Jewellery & accessories.
- Home décor / furnishings / gifting.
- Bakeries, cloud kitchens, sweet shops (photogenic, repeat customers).

**Qualifying traits:**
- Owner-operated or ≤10 staff.
- Already has a customer phone list (informal is fine — a notebook, old orders, a "broadcast list").
- Already posts product photos somewhere (Instagram, WhatsApp Status, Facebook).
- Sells repeat-purchase or seasonal items (reason to message customers again).

**Explicitly out of scope for now:** large D2C brands (AiSensy/Interakt own them), services with no product photo, anyone needing complex chatbots.

---

## 3. Where to find them (ranked by speed)

1. **Your own network first.** Family, friends, neighborhood shops you buy from. 5 warm intros beats 50 cold DMs for the first week. Ask each one: "who else runs a shop like yours?"
2. **Instagram.** Search hashtags + location: `#sareeshop`, `#boutique<city>`, `#jewellerydesigner`, `#homedecorindia`. DM owners who post products regularly but have small followings (real SMBs, not influencers).
3. **Local markets in person.** A morning walking a textile/jewellery market with a phone demo converts far better than online. Bring the live demo on your phone.
4. **WhatsApp/Facebook seller groups & local business associations** (market traders' associations, "<city> small business owners").
5. **Marketplace seller communities** — Meesho/Amazon Karigar/ONDC seller groups, local Shopify/Dukaan store owners.
6. **Resellers/agencies** who already set up WhatsApp for shops — they're a distribution channel *and* a source of intros (see GTM plan).

Target: **line up 12–15 conversations** to get 12 good ones.

---

## 4. The interview (keep it ~20 min, mostly listening)

**Rules:** Ask about their life and past behavior, not your idea. Don't pitch until the end. Don't ask "would you use this?" — ask "what did you do last time?" No leading questions.

### Warm-up (2 min)
- "Tell me about your shop — what do you sell, who buys from you?"
- "How do regulars find out when you have something new?"

### Problem & current behavior (8 min) — the core
- "Walk me through the last time you told customers about a new product or offer. What exactly did you do?"
- "How long did that take? Who did it?"
- "How did you decide what to write? Where did the photo come from?"
- "How many people did it reach? How do you know if it worked?"
- "What's the most annoying part of that whole thing?"
- "Have you ever paid for any tool or person to help with this?" (probe: how much, still using it, why/why not)

### Magnitude & money (4 min)
- "How often do you want to message customers — weekly, festivals only, never?"
- "If this took 2 minutes instead of [their answer], what would that be worth to you per month?"
- "What would have to be true for you to pay for something like this?"

### The wedge test (4 min) — show, don't tell
- Open the live demo on your phone. Photograph one of *their* products on the spot. Generate the campaign in front of them.
- Watch their face. Say nothing for 5 seconds.
- "What's your honest first reaction?" → "What's missing or wrong?" → "Would you send this to your customers as-is?"

### Close (2 min)
- "Can I set you up as one of our first 5 shops? It's free to start." (gauge real commitment)
- "Who else should I show this to?"

---

## 5. Signals to record after each chat

Score each interview 1–5 on:
- **Problem intensity** — did they light up / complain unprompted about marketing effort?
- **Current spend/effort** — are they already spending time or money on this?
- **Wedge reaction** — visceral positive to the photo→campaign moment?
- **Pay intent** — vague "nice" vs. "how much / can I start now"?
- **Referral** — did they offer to introduce others?

**Strong buy signals:** they ask the price; they ask "can I use it now"; they immediately think of which product to send; they offer a referral; they try to keep the demo output.

**Red flags / disconfirming:** "I'll think about it"; can't recall the last time they marketed; no customer list; only care about acquiring *new* customers via ads (that's the AiSensy ad motion, not our owned-audience wedge — note it, it changes positioning).

Keep a simple tracker (a Google Sheet or the spreadsheet I can generate): name, shop type, city, scores, verbatim quotes, commitment, referrals.

---

## 6. Decision after the sprint

- **≥ validation bar (8/12 + 5 wedge + 3 pay):** double down — onboard the 3–5 committed shops for real (see FIRST-RETAILER-ONBOARDING.md), start the distribution plan.
- **Mixed (problem yes, wedge meh):** the problem is real but the photo wedge isn't the hook — iterate the demo/positioning before scaling.
- **Weak problem signal:** pivot the ICP (try a different vertical) or the value prop before building more.

The cost of this sprint is ~2 weeks and your time. The cost of skipping it is months building the wrong thing.
